**Masanori Ota** (太田正典, _Ōta Masanori_; born November 23, 1961), better known by his [pen name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_name "Pen name") **Masamune Shirow** (士郎 正宗, _Shirō Masamune_), is a Japanese [manga artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga_artist "Manga artist").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masamune_Shirow#cite_note-1) Shirow is best known for the manga _[Ghost in the Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_in_the_Shell_(manga) "Ghost in the Shell (manga)")_, which has since been turned into three theatrical [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") films, two anime television series, an anime television film, an anime [ONA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_net_animation "Original net animation") series, a theatrical [live action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_action "Live action") film, and several [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"), as well as being a key figure in the development of [cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk") aesthetics and themes in Japan during the 1980s and 1990s.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masamune_Shirow#cite_note-:0-2)

## Life and career

Born in the [Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture "Hyōgo Prefecture") capital city of [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe,_Hyogo "Kobe, Hyogo"), he studied oil painting at [Osaka University of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University_of_Arts "Osaka University of Arts"). While in college, he developed an interest in [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga "Manga"), which led him to create his own complete work, _[Black Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Magic_M-66 "Black Magic M-66")_, which was published in the manga _[dōjinshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Djinshi "Dōjinshi")_ _Atlas_. His work caught the eye of [Seishinsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Seishinsha&action=edit&redlink=1 "Seishinsha (page does not exist)") [[jp](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92%E5%BF%83%E7%A4%BE "jp:青心社")] President Harumichi Aoki, who offered to publish him.

The result was best-selling[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masamune_Shirow#cite_note-3) manga _[Appleseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleseed_(manga) "Appleseed (manga)")_, a full volume of densely plotted drama taking place in an ambiguous future. The story was a sensation, and won the 1986 [Seiun Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiun_Award "Seiun Award") for Best Manga. After a professional reprint of _Black Magic_ and a second volume of _Appleseed_, he released _[Dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_(manga) "Dominion (manga)")_ in 1986. Two more volumes of _Appleseed_ followed before he began work on _[Ghost in the Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_in_the_Shell "Ghost in the Shell")_. His work has been cited as an influence on [The Wachowskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wachowskis "The Wachowskis") and the development of the popular [Matrix film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Matrix_(franchise) "The Matrix (franchise)"). [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masamune_Shirow#cite_note-:0-2)

In 2007, he collaborated again with [Production I.G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_I.G "Production I.G") to co-create the original concept for the anime television series _[Ghost Hound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Hound "Ghost Hound")_, Production I.G's 20th anniversary project. A further original collaboration with Production I.G began airing in April 2008, titled _[Real Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Drive "Real Drive")_. His work often explores the philosophical themes of [cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk") such as the nature of [post-human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumanism "Posthumanism") existence, [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") and the ethics of biotechnology, human–machine relations, [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism"), and [globalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalism "Globalism"). [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masamune_Shirow#cite_note-:0-2)

### Galgrease
_Galgrease_ (published in _Uppers Magazine_, 2002) is the collected name of several hentai manga and poster books by Shirow. The name comes from the fact that the women depicted often look "greased".

The first series of _Galgrease_ booklets included four issues each in the following settings:  
* _Wild Wet West_ ([Wild West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_West "Wild West")-themed)  
* _Hellhound_ ([Horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_(genre) "Horror (genre)")-themed)  
* _Galhound_ (Near-future [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction")–themed)

The second series included another run of 12 booklets in the following worlds:  
* _Wild Wet Quest_ (A _[Tomb Raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_Raider "Tomb Raider")_ or _[Indiana Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Jones "Indiana Jones")_–style sequel to _Wild Wet West_)  
* _Hellcat_ ([Pirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate "Pirate")-themed)  
* _Galhound 2_ (Near-future [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction")–themed)
After each regular series, there were one or more bonus poster books that revisited the existing characters and settings.
